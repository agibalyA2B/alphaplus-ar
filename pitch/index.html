What the transport rules do—and don’t—say

AED 100 for eating or drinking in prohibited areas; AED 200 for compromising cleanliness. This pitch is about carrying a sealed vessel, not drinking on the train. Selling coffee in a station is not new: cafés already trade in station units before the ticket gates, and a convenience chain sells fresh food and drinks at four Green Line stations. The rules bite past the gates, which is why the vessel seals. The fine schedule has no provision against carrying a closed non-alcoholic drink (our reading of Schedule 3).

01 / AT THE BARISTA

HOLD→SEAL

Buy. Fill.
Snap breakfast on.

The barista fills and seals your coffee in about 20 seconds (espresso or Americano, no milk). A pastry pod snaps underneath. No extra paper bag. A tap of the vessel’s NFC tag links it to your payment.

One purchase. One sealed carry.

Source: Team 4 service design; about 20 seconds is a rough team test, timed properly in the pilot.

Two hands.
Both free.

Carry on board. Don’t consume on board.

Source: Team 4 concept; clip retention and sealing tested in the pilot.

Feasibility means testing the system, not just the cup

The pilot must test leak resistance, strap-clip retention, food-safe separation, hot-liquid handling, wash validation, return logistics, deposit acceptance, and the deposit hold and refund through the kiosk’s card terminal, matched to each vessel by its NFC tag. The CAD demonstrates form and fit, not a production-ready design or safety certification.

The environmental case is conditional

40–50 uses

For a steel cup to beat a paper cup on CO₂

Return is the point.
Measure it.

Reuse is not automatically lower-carbon. Vessel life, washing, transport and losses all matter. Our pilot must measure the whole loop.

Less cup and bag waste from day one, and a CO₂ reduction as returns climb. A stainless steel cup needs about 40 to 50 uses to break even on climate impact (CIRAIG 2014, reported by CHOICE). Each vessel lasts on average 1 ÷ (1 − return rate) uses: about 10 at our 90% pilot target. The pilot measures how much CO₂ the loop saves.

Sources: CHOICE; RECYC-QUÉBEC / CIRAIG summary. Return-rate conversion: Team 4 model.

Open the model assumptions, not just the headline

Model v5 is a three-year cash flow at a 10% discount rate, with no terminal value. Base inputs: AED 17 menu price including VAT; ingredients 26% of sales; vessel AED 15.14 blended (AED 17.77 for the first 10,000, AED 14.83 after moulds); 90% return rate (team pilot target); AED 26 deposit; AED 125k setup per kiosk; AED 100k tooling.

One change at a time: AED 16 menu price, NPV +2.11M; ingredients at 35% of sales, +1.12M; no lost-vessel deposits kept, +1.08M. These figures support a course recommendation, not an investment offer.

External evidence

  1. Dubai Media Office / RTA, 2 August 2026 — Dubai Metro carried 136.5 million rides from January to June 2026 (39.2% of 348.1 million across all modes). Busiest stations: BurJuman 8.4M, Union 6.5M, Al Rigga 6.4M. Daily figures are Team 4 calculations over 181 days. Rides, not unique people.
  2. Executive Council Resolution No. 3 of 2026 — prohibited eating/drinking and cleanliness fines. Carrying a sealed vessel is not represented as an offence.
  3. Station precedent — cafés trading in station units before the gates (Sharetea, BurJuman Exit 3; Sunda Café and Alcatraz Café, Emirates Towers; Slims Café, Dubai Internet City; Tea Station, Al Furjan; Google Maps listings, 30 Oct 2026). Gulf News (Aug 2026): Choithrams Quickerr sells fresh food, grab-and-go and beverages at Abu Hail, Stadium, Al Nahda and DAFZA stations. Khaleej Times (Oct 2025): RTA station units are listed with rent, power and the activities not allowed. Operator-reported, informal: station coffee kiosks at BurJuman and Al Rigga take about 400 orders on a weekday.
  4. Environmental benchmark — CHOICE reports an approximate 40-use stainless-steel break-even. The CIRAIG 2014 report distinguishes impact categories (50 uses for climate change versus 40 for resources in its average disposable-cup-with-lid comparison). The commissioning body’s summary limits energy-context results to Québec. Team 4 converts uses into a return rate with average uses = 1 ÷ (1 − r): 40 uses is about 97.5%, 50 uses is 98%. This modelling assumption treats non-returns as the only way a vessel is lost.
